GIFs get big fast. 480 px wide at 12 fps is a good balance. Keep clips short: a few seconds is ideal.

How it works

  1. Add your video

    Drop in an MP4, MOV or WebM. It opens in your browser, not on a server.

  2. Pick the moment

    Scrub to the start and end, then choose the frame rate and width.

  3. Make and download

    We grab each frame, build the GIF and give you the file.

Good to know. There’s a 600 frame cap, which is 50 seconds at 12 fps, because longer GIFs get huge and slow every browser down. If your browser can’t play a file (some iPhone HEVC clips on Windows), it can’t turn it into a GIF either. Convert it to a standard MP4 first.

Questions

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. The frames are read and the GIF is built inside your browser tab. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

What frame rate should I use?

10 to 15 fps looks smooth for most clips. Go lower for smaller files, higher for fast motion. Every extra frame adds to the size.

Why is my GIF so big?

GIFs store every frame as a picture with up to 256 colours, so they don’t compress like video. Make it narrower, shorter or lower the frame rate, or run it through our GIF optimiser.

Does it keep the sound?

No. GIFs can’t hold sound. If you need sound, keep it as a video and use our trim tool instead.
